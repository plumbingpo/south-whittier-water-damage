Crawl Space Water Removal: What South Whittier Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Warning Signs

  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Air From Below

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Weakened Floor Framing

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.
