Attic Water Damage Help in South Whittier

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In South Whittier, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

A Falling Ceiling

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

What We Do

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.
