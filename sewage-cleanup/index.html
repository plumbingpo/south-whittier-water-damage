A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in South Whittier, South Whittier Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:
