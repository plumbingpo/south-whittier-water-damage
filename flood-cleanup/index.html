How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Heavy rainfall, a dead sump pump and water spreading across the basement
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Rainwater getting inside through a roof or wall damaged by a storm
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source

Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Debris in a gutter or storm drain blocks proper flow, so clean them out regularly
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Extend your downspouts to carry rain from the roof out past the foundation
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Seal foundation cracks and apply a waterproofing coating to basement walls
