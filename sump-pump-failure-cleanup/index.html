Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in South Whittier: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Basement Floor Coverings

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Furnace and Water Heater

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Wall Framing

The wood plate at the base of each wall sits right on the slab and soaks up water first. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.
  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • Ongoing seepage that requires us to run a temporary pump in the pit.

What We Do

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.
