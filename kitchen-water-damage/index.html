Kitchen Water Damage in South Whittier

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. We help South Whittier homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

The Dishwasher Cavity

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
