Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in South Whittier

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In South Whittier, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. Clean water means extraction and drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
How We Fix It

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

Every fixture, the floor, the subfloor and the base of each wall gets cleaned and receives an antimicrobial treatment. For Category 3 jobs, disinfection comes first and drying equipment starts only afterward.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Flushed wipes, excess paper or a small toy caught in the trap.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.

What the Water Damages

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Lingering Odor

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Subfloor Saturation

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.
